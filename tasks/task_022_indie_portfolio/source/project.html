Tinybase

A 6kb local-first state library for React. 4,200 stars, 47k weekly downloads.

Role — Solo author & maintainer Year — 2022 → present Status — v2.3 stable License — MIT
problem

The gap between Redux and useState.

For three years I kept building the same thing in every offline-first React app I shipped: a thin reactive store that survived reloads, synced across tabs, and didn't make me write seventeen action creators to update a boolean. Redux Toolkit had become defensible at scale but punishing at small. useState was lovely until you needed two components to agree on anything.

Zustand and Jotai got close, but neither shipped persistence or sync as first-class concerns — and the moment you reached for IndexedDB you were back to writing the same 400 lines of boilerplate. Tinybase exists to make local-first state the default, not a side quest.

approach

Three layers, no providers.

Signals-based reactivity. Under the hood, every value is a fine-grained signal. Components subscribe only to the keys they read, so a single field update never re-renders the tree. The public API still looks like a hook — useStore(s => s.user.name) — but the diffing happens at the source.

IndexedDB persistence layer. A pluggable adapter flushes mutations to IndexedDB in a debounced microtask. Reads on first paint are warm because the store hydrates synchronously from a tiny in-memory snapshot kept in sessionStorage. Total cold-start overhead: ~2ms on a mid-2020 MBA.

Optional CRDT sync adapter. Last year I extracted the sync engine into syncd, a separate package. Bring your own server, or use the hosted relay. Conflict resolution is Yjs-compatible, which means you can drop Tinybase into a doc collaboration app without rewriting your data model.

decisions

Things I'd defend at a whiteboard.

  • Why no Provider

    Providers force a tree structure on what is fundamentally a singleton. They make testing harder, SSR weirder, and they bloat the bundle for no observed benefit. The store is module-scoped; if you need isolation in tests, create a new store.

  • Why signals over reducers

    Reducers are great when state transitions are the interesting object. For 90% of UI work, the interesting object is the value. Signals make the dependency graph explicit, which means we can sub-render with surgical precision and skip 80% of the reconciliation cost.

  • Why I rewrote it in TypeScript at v2

    v1 was JS with .d.ts files hand-maintained alongside. By month four I was making three commits per change. The TS rewrite added 11kb of source but zero runtime bytes — and the inferred types caught two latent bugs in the persistence adapter on day one.
