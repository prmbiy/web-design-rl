Our Story

A quiet practice in the heart of the city.

Six years of slow mornings, candlelit evenings, and the steady company of breath. This is how Stillwater began — and how it continues.

Founding

How Stillwater began.

Mira Halvorsen opened the doors of Stillwater on a quiet morning in September 2018, the way she opens every class — without fanfare, with a long exhale. For ten years before that, she had been teaching: first in a small zendo in Kyoto, where she studied the slow architecture of Iyengar alignment and learned to sit; later in a converted printing house in Greenpoint, where her Saturday classes ran past their hour because no one wanted to leave the floor.

What she wanted to build, she said later, was not a studio so much as a room. A room with reclaimed oak under your feet, linen at the windows, and enough quiet that you could hear your own breath find you. A practice that did not perform itself. A place where beginners and lifelong students sat on the same warm wood and started, again, from where they were.

Stillwater is that room. It has grown — twelve teachers now, a tea bar, an online streaming archive, retreats in Costa Rica and the Hudson Valley — but its center has not. The doors still open at six. The floor is still warm. Mira still teaches Tuesdays.

Philosophy

Three things we keep returning to.

I.

Slowness

We move at the pace of breath, not the pace of the clock. The body has its own time.

II.

Lineage

We honor the teachers who came before us — and the rooms in Kyoto, Pune, and Brooklyn that shaped this practice.

III.

Belonging

The mat is for every body. There is no shape you have to make to be welcome here.

The Space

1,800 sq ft of breath.

The studio sits on the second floor of a converted warehouse on Wythe Avenue. Tall east-facing windows fill the room with morning light. Reclaimed oak floors, warmed by underfloor heating in the colder months. Linen curtains we open and close with the seasons.

At the back, a small tea bar serves matcha, hojicha, and a rotating list of herbal blends from a tea grower in upstate New York. Students often linger after class, kneeling on the bench, watching the steam rise. We think this is part of the practice too.

Community Giving

The Karma Class.

Every Sunday morning, we hold a donation-based class open to all. One hundred percent of the proceeds go to The Loveland Foundation, which provides therapy and healing resources for Black women and girls.

The class is taught on rotation by our full teaching staff. There is no minimum donation. Bring a friend, bring your stillness, bring whatever you can.

Beginning

If you are new here.

01
What should I bring to my first class?
Comfortable clothes you can move and bend in, and a water bottle if you'd like. Mats, blocks, bolsters, and blankets are all provided. We rinse and care for them between classes.
02
When should I arrive?
Please arrive ten to fifteen minutes before class. This gives you time to settle, find your spot, and meet the teacher. Doors close gently as class begins.
03
I've never done yoga before. Is that okay?
More than okay. Our Slow Flow and Yin classes are well-suited to beginners, and our teachers are practiced at offering quiet adjustments and options for every body in the room.
04
Is there an etiquette I should know?
Phones rest in the cubbies by the door. We move slowly between poses and keep voices soft in the lobby. If you arrive after class begins, please join us at the next opening rather than during a held pose.
05
How does pricing work?
Drop-in classes are $28. We offer an 8-Class Pack at $168, a Studio Unlimited membership at $189/month, and Stillwater Stream — our online library — at $24/month. Your first class is always $15.
06
What if I need to cancel a class?
We ask for twelve hours' notice so the spot can find another practitioner. Late cancellations count as a class against your pack, but we are kind about life — write to us if something unusual comes up.
