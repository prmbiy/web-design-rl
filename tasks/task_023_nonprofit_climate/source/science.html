Plain language

Five key findings from IPCC AR6.

The Sixth Assessment Report is the most comprehensive scientific consensus ever assembled on Earth's climate. Here's what it actually says.

FINDING 01

Human influence is unequivocal.

For the first time, the IPCC drops all hedging language. Greenhouse gas emissions from human activity have warmed the atmosphere, ocean, and land. There is no longer a credible alternative explanation.

FINDING 02

The 1.5°C carbon budget is nearly spent.

At current emissions of ~40 GtCO₂/year, the remaining budget for a 50% chance of limiting warming to 1.5°C will be exhausted in approximately 5–7 years. Every ton matters.

FINDING 03

Tipping points are closer than thought.

Five major tipping elements — Greenland ice sheet, West Antarctic ice sheet, Amazon rainforest, coral reefs, and boreal permafrost — could be triggered between 1.1°C and 1.5°C of warming.

FINDING 04

Adaptation has hard limits.

Some impacts — saltwater intrusion, glacier loss, species extinction — cannot be adapted to. Adaptation buys time but is not a substitute for cutting emissions at the source.

FINDING 05

Solutions exist and are cost-effective.

Solar and wind are now the cheapest sources of new electricity in 90% of the world. Cutting emissions in half by 2030 is technically and economically feasible — what's missing is political will.

FINDING 06

Climate justice is not optional.

The countries least responsible for emissions face the worst impacts first. AR6 explicitly frames equitable adaptation finance as a precondition for effective global action.

Common Questions

Addressing misconceptions.

Click any question to expand. Each answer is reviewed by our Scientific Advisory Board.

Isn't the climate always changing? Why blame humans now?
Yes, Earth's climate has changed before — over thousands to millions of years, driven by orbital cycles, volcanic activity, and solar variation. What's different now is the rate and the cause. The current warming is occurring 10× faster than at any point in the last 65 million years, and the chemical fingerprint of the extra CO₂ in our atmosphere shows it came from burning fossil fuels.
What about volcanoes? Don't they emit more CO₂ than humans?
No — this is one of the most persistent myths. According to the USGS, all the world's volcanoes combined emit roughly 0.3 gigatons of CO₂ per year. Human activity emits approximately 40 gigatons per year — more than 130 times as much.
Won't technology — carbon capture, geoengineering — save us?
Direct air capture and CCS are part of the toolkit, but they currently capture roughly 0.01% of annual emissions and require enormous energy inputs. The cheapest, fastest carbon to remove is the carbon we never emit. Solar, wind, electrification, and efficiency together can deliver 70% of the needed reductions by 2030.
Is it already too late to do anything?
No — and this framing is itself a form of climate denial. Every tenth of a degree of avoided warming reduces deaths, displacement, and species loss. The difference between 1.5°C and 2°C means hundreds of millions more people exposed to extreme heat, and the loss of virtually all coral reefs. The fight is over how bad it gets, not whether to fight.
Aren't individual actions pointless against corporations?
Individual lifestyle changes alone won't solve climate change — but framing it as "individual vs. systemic" is a false choice. The most powerful individual actions are political: voting, organizing, joining campaigns, talking to neighbors, supporting frontline groups. Our Take Action page lists six high-leverage options.
If global temperature has only risen 1.2°C, why does it feel so much worse?
1.2°C is a global average — but warming is not evenly distributed. Land warms about 1.6× faster than ocean, and the Arctic is warming nearly 4× faster than the global average. Locally, this translates into much larger swings in extreme heat, drought, and rainfall.
How can scientists predict 2100 when they can't predict next week's weather?
Weather and climate are different problems. Weather is the day-to-day state of the atmosphere; climate is the long-term statistical pattern. We can't tell you if it will rain on November 14th, 2087 — but we can tell you with high confidence that the 2080s will be hotter, on average, than the 2020s, the same way we know summer is warmer than winter.
What about cosmic rays, the sun, or natural cycles?
Solar output has been measured directly by satellites since 1978 and has been slightly declining while temperatures climb sharply. Cosmic ray flux has shown no correlation with the recent warming trend. Every major natural driver has been investigated and ruled out as the cause of current warming. Only greenhouse gas forcing fits the data.
