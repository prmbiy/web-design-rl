About amoxicillin

Amoxicillin is a penicillin antibiotic. It's used to treat bacterial infections, such as chest infections (including pneumonia) and dental abscesses.

It can also be used together with other antibiotics and medicines to treat stomach ulcers caused by H. pylori infection.

Key facts

  • Take amoxicillin at evenly spaced times throughout the day.
  • Common side effects include feeling sick (nausea) and diarrhoea.
  • Finish the full course of medicine even if you feel better.
  • Liquid amoxicillin can stain your teeth — this does not last and is removed by brushing.
  • Amoxicillin is generally safe to take during pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

Who can and cannot take amoxicillin

Amoxicillin can be taken by adults, including pregnant and breastfeeding women, and children.

Amoxicillin is not suitable for everyone. Tell your doctor before starting the medicine if you:

  • have ever had an allergic reaction to amoxicillin or any other medicine
  • have liver or kidney problems
  • have recently had, or are about to have, any vaccinations

How and when to take amoxicillin

The usual dose of amoxicillin capsules is 250mg to 500mg taken 3 times a day. The dose may be lower for children.

  1. Swallow capsules whole with a drink of water. Do not chew or break them.
  2. Space your doses evenly throughout the day — try to take them every 8 hours.
  3. You can take amoxicillin with or without food. For liquid medicine, use the plastic syringe or spoon that comes with it.
  4. Carry on taking the medicine until you've completed the course, even if you feel better. Stopping early can make the infection come back.
  5. If you forget a dose, take it as soon as you remember — unless your next dose is due in less than 4 hours, in which case skip the missed dose.
⚠ Important

Allergic reactions — when to call 999

In rare cases, amoxicillin can cause a serious allergic reaction (anaphylaxis).

Call 999 or go to A&E now if:

  • your lips, mouth, throat or tongue suddenly become swollen
  • you're breathing very fast or struggling to breathe (you may become very wheezy or feel like you're choking)
  • your throat feels tight or you're struggling to swallow
  • your skin, tongue or lips turn blue, grey or pale
  • you suddenly become very confused, drowsy or dizzy
  • someone faints and cannot be woken up
  • a child is limp, floppy or not responding like they normally do

You or the person who's unwell may also have a rash that's swollen, raised, itchy, blistered or peeling.

Side effects

Like all medicines, amoxicillin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.

Common side effects

These common side effects of amoxicillin happen in more than 1 in 100 people. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist if these side effects bother you or do not go away:

  • feeling sick (nausea)
  • diarrhoea
  • thrush (a yeast infection in the vagina, mouth or under foreskin)
  • headache
  • mild skin rash

Serious side effects

It happens rarely, but some people have serious side effects after taking amoxicillin.

Call a doctor or call 111 straight away if you get:

  • diarrhoea (sometimes with stomach cramps) that contains blood or mucus — if you have severe diarrhoea for more than 4 days
  • pale poo with dark pee, yellowing of the whites of your eyes or your skin (jaundice)
  • bruising or skin discolouration
  • joint or muscle pain that comes on after 2 days of taking the medicine
  • a skin rash with circular red patches

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Amoxicillin is generally considered safe to take during pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

For more information about how amoxicillin can affect you and your baby, read the leaflet about amoxicillin on the Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy (BUMPS) website.

Cautions with other medicines

There are some medicines that may affect how amoxicillin works, or that amoxicillin itself may affect.

Medicines that interact with amoxicillin
Medicine Effect of the interaction
Methotrexate (used to treat arthritis and psoriasis) Amoxicillin can increase methotrexate levels, raising the risk of side effects.
Warfarin (a blood thinner) May increase the risk of bleeding. Extra INR monitoring may be needed.
The contraceptive pill If you experience sickness or diarrhoea, the pill may not work properly — use extra contraception.
Allopurinol (used for gout) Taking this with amoxicillin may increase the chance of an allergic skin rash.
Other antibiotics (e.g. tetracyclines, chloramphenicol) May reduce the effectiveness of amoxicillin.
Typhoid vaccine (oral) Amoxicillin can stop the typhoid vaccine working — tell your nurse or doctor.

Tell your doctor or pharmacist if you're taking any other medicines, including herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.

Common questions

How long does amoxicillin take to work?

For most infections, you should feel better within a few days. It's very important you keep taking amoxicillin until your course is finished.

Will it affect my contraception?

Amoxicillin does not stop the contraceptive pill working. However, if it makes you sick or gives you severe diarrhoea for more than 24 hours, your contraceptive pills may not protect you from pregnancy.

Can I drink alcohol with it?

Yes, you can drink alcohol while taking amoxicillin.

Get urgent help

When to get help straight away

For a serious allergic reaction (anaphylaxis), call 999 or go to A&E immediately.
