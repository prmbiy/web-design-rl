Signal Drift · Episode 142

The Last Search Engine

47:22 runtime Released Nov 14, 2024 Hosted by Maya Okafor Guest: Aanya Krishnan
— Episode notes

The general-purpose web search engine, that strange utility we once treated as a public good, is quietly dying. Spam farms, SEO arbitrage, and AI slop have hollowed out the ten blue links until they read like a satellite radio loop of advertorial. This week on Signal Drift, Maya sits down with search engineer Aanya Krishnan to ask what happens after the index breaks.

The conversation winds from the original PageRank paper to the strange flourishing of small, weird search engines: Marginalia, which indexes the non-commercial web; Kagi, which sells search the way magazines used to be sold; and a handful of vertical indexes for academic papers, recipes, and old forums. Aanya, who spent six years at DuckDuckGo before walking away, argues that "general search" was always a brief commercial accident, not a natural state of the web.

We close with listener mail, including a long letter from a librarian in Wellington about whether finding things online is now closer to dowsing than retrieval.

"The miracle wasn't that Google indexed the web. The miracle was that, for about fifteen years, the web was small enough and honest enough to be indexed at all." — Aanya Krishnan, 34:08
