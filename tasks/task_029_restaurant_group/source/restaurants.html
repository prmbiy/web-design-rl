Otsu by Verleux

A ten-seat kaiseki counter built around a single fire and the morning's market.

Eight courses change nightly. Sourcing is direct from Toyosu twice weekly, with vegetable provenance shared with our Hudson Valley restaurants. The room is hushed, the lacquerware antique, the rice from a single field in Niigata.

Atelier Hudson — main barn
Hudson, NY · Modern American

Atelier Hudson

Wood-fired Modern American in a restored 1840s dairy barn overlooking the Hudson River.

Two New York Times stars and an obsessive relationship with the farms within a fifteen-mile radius. The hearth burns local apple and hickory; the cellar leans toward Finger Lakes Riesling and obscure Loire reds. A long lunch on the porch is among our quietest pleasures.

Chef de CuisineMaya Okonkwo

Cobble Hill · French Bistro

Le Petit Verleux

The original Verleux room — a thirty-eight seat bistro running steadily since 2014.

Zinc bar, red banquettes, and a daily $58 three-course prix fixe served 5–7pm. The dessert list has not changed in eight years and that is on purpose. The natural-wine list is short, French, and revised quietly each season by our cellar master.

Bar Maribel — after midnight
Williamsburg · Spanish Small Plates

Bar Maribel

A late-night Spanish bar with one of the city's deepest natural-wine lists from Galicia, Jerez, and Catalunya.

Service runs until 1am Thursday through Saturday. The bar is high, the music is loud, the jamón is sliced thinly and to order. Half of the menu changes weekly; the Gilda has been the same since opening night and always will be.

Verleux Pasta Bar

A sixteen-seat counter built around an open pasta laboratory and a single bronze die extruder.

Six pastas nightly, hand-cut in front of you, plus a daily fresh sheet of seafood from Montauk. Best enjoyed at the counter with a Lambrusco and a long view of flour, eggs, and patience. Walk-ins welcome but always a wait.
